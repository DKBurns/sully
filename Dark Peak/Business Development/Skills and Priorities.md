# Skills and priorities

Sully Chowdhury, October 2026. For Darren.

A ranking of what I want more of and less of, and what that means for my week.

## 1. Stated preference research

DCE, BWS, TTO, vignette-based valuation, and extending SEEP to support them.

The one I most want to be known for. It's the only area where I'm the resident expert rather than the person catching up, it's years of work from OHE, and I know where the existing software falls short because I hit those limits myself. Jane's interest on the TTO and vignette side suggests there's genuine client pull rather than it just being an interest of mine.

Day to day this looks like: method design, the DCE and TTO engines in SEEP, the business case, and the client conversations that come with it.

## 2. Advanced modelling

Microsimulation, DES, survival methods, and the statistical depth underneath the models rather than just implementing them.

High enjoyment and I don't want to lose it. There are also real gaps I want to close on methods I've inherited rather than built, particularly the statistical work like risk equations, meta-regression and ITC. I'd rather learn them properly than keep working around them. Involved in one stats project a year. 

## 3. Development and full stack

Working with Jesus on SEEP: the Vue client, the node side, Docker, the R layer and other software platforms. 

Not something I expected to be doing when I joined, and more useful than I expected. It's also what makes the platform half of the preference work real rather than theoretical, so it compounds with the first item.

## 4. Delivery, coordination and client work

Project management, billing, country adaptations, the day-to-day client channel.

I'm good at this and I'll carry it. It isn't what I'd pick personally but its valuable to a company, and I'd want it sized so it doesn't absorb the technical time.

## 5. Admin

Low priority, batched.

## Data science, separately

Python, SQL, ML, data engineering.

This sits outside the ranking because it isn't a preference in the same way. I want it because the combination of health economics, modelling and real data science is rare, and because working with health data properly is more interesting than working at the edges of it. It's stayed theoretical for over a year while everything else has moved.

The ask is : one or two projects a year with genuine engineering or ML content, something that runs and that I can point to. Not a day a week because I know these projects are probably harder to come by. Though I do take the "data scientist" part of my job title seriously and do want to show more of it that isnt just modelling. 

## What I'd trade

Preferences without trades aren't much use, as we know. 

If protected technical and preference-work time meant handing the AZ coordination layer and the day-to-day client channel to someone else, I'd take that trade. I'd stay on the technical side and on the client relationships that matter to the preference work, but I don't need to be the person holding the action list.

I'd also take responsibility for one or two junior persons rather than a team. I like seeing someone develop and I'd want to do that properly. Managing a larger group interests me less.

## What my week would ideally look like

- Two days as a floor for deep technical and preference work, modelling and statistical  work, protected rather than aspirational.
- One day on SEEP and the platform side.
- (If possible) One day on data science stuff outside of modelling e.g. Setting up data pipelines, setting up automated visualisations etc to replace the below when/if those sorts of projects happen
- One day on client facing stuff or shorter requests
- One day absorbing everything else: proposals, reviews, capacity.
- A short daily window for small things so they don't fragment the protected days.

## What I'm less suited to

Constant business development (my social batteries drain) and constant new client acquisition. Large-team management. A permanently reactive week (I find that stressful though its hard to avoid sometimes unfortunately).
